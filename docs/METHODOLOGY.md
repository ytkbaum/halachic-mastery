# Methodology — How the Map Is Weighted

This document defines how every topic in the Halachic Mastery System is scored, organized, and personalized. It is the shared language for any discussion about the map.

---

## Two Questions for Every Item

| # | Question | What it determines |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How urgent is it to know?** | A weighted sum of seven 1–5 axes |
| 2 | **How should it be held?** | **Master** (learn thoroughly) or **Recognize & ask a rav** (know enough to spot the situation and bring the question) |

---

## The Seven Axes

Each axis is scored 1–5 and carries an editable weight. The composite score, tier, and rank are all computed by formula — adjust a weight and the entire map re-sorts. This is deliberate: the framework should be tunable to a community or a point of view, not lock in a single opinion.

| Axis | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Frequency (act)** | How often the act is performed or the occasion arises |
| **Ongoing relevance** | How continuously the item bears on life, even when the act itself is rare |
| **Error-likelihood** | How often people get it wrong in practice |
| **Awareness-gap** | How many do not realize it is a mitzvah or issur at all |
| **Centrality** | Foundational weight in Jewish life, independent of how well-known it is |
| **Severity** | Gravity of the matter (see [Severity model](#severity-model) below) |
| **Real-time stakes** | Whether it must be acted on immediately, with no time to ask a rav |

### Tiers

The composite score maps to one of five tiers:

**Foundational** → **High** → **Moderate** → **Situational** → **Rare**

---

## Levels of Obligation

Every item carries its level of obligation — *d'oraysa*, *d'rabbanan*, custom, or ethical — kept visible alongside its score.

This is not only a ranking input; it is a **teaching goal**: cultivating a calibrated sense of weight, so the biblical is never treated lightly and a stringency is never mistaken for the essence.

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| *D'oraysa* | Biblical obligation |
| *D'rabbanan* | Rabbinic enactment |
| Custom | Established practice (*minhag*) |
| Ethical | Middot, character, and interpersonal norms |

---

## Severity Model

Severity is the gravity of the matter — one of the seven scored axes:

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| **5** | *Kares*, court-administered *misah*, or a cardinal sin |
| **4** | *Malkos* / *misah biydei shamayim* |
| **3** | An ordinary biblical prohibition |
| **2** | Rabbinic |
| **1** | Stringency or good practice |

> Punishment detail (*kares*, *misah* and its type) is drawn from the source text where it states it, and left blank where the text is silent. Nothing is invented.

## Tracks

The corpus is divided into thematic tracks — coherent domains, each able to anchor one day of a weekly cycle. **Triage happens within each track**, so a learner advances across all of them in parallel and reaches the most crucial matter in every domain early, rather than waiting on a topic's place in a sequence.

### Seed Track Set

| Track | Domain |
|---|---|
| 🙏 Emunah & Trust | Faith, bitachon, and relationship with Hashem |
| 🕯️ Daily Avodah | Tefillah, berachos, and daily practice |
| ✡️ Shabbos & Moadim | Weekly and annual cycle |
| 🗣️ Speech & Middos | Lashon hara, emes, character |
| ⚖️ Mamon & Mishpat | Business, finance, and interpersonal law |
| 🍽️ Food, Land & Gifts | Kashrut, agricultural mitzvos, tzedakah |
| 🕍 Kedushah & Guarding | Taharat hamishpacha, tznius, and related areas |

## The Living Mitzvah (Derivatives)

Many formal mitzvos are rare as acts but constant as living forms. The framework unbinds them: the **formal mitzvah keeps its true (low) frequency**, while its common everyday derivative becomes its own scored entry.

| Formal mitzvah (rare) | Living form (common) |
|---|---|
| A formal oath | Casual swearing — *"I swear"* |
| Affixing a mezuzah | Engaging it mindfully at every doorway |
| Consulting a medium (*ov*) | Trust in Hashem against omens and superstition |

This keeps the prevalent everyday practice from being buried under the rare formal act.

## The Learner Layer

Prioritization says *what* and *when* within a domain. The learner layer says *for whom*, *how*, and *when in a person's growth*.

### Levels

Four stages, each with its focus and ideal mode of learning. Every item is tagged with the level at which it first enters the ideal curriculum:

| Level | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Foundations** | Core obligations, daily practice, high-stakes awareness |
| **Building** | Broader coverage, common edge cases, prerequisite chains |
| **Maturing** | Depth, nuance, less-frequent but important areas |
| **Comprehensive** | Full coverage, rare situations, advanced detail |

### Personalization

An overlay of personal attributes — marital status, profession, areas of struggle — that brings the halachos *a given life turns on* toward the front.

> Grounded in *Mesillas Yesharim* (ch. 26): piety is one, but its path differs with each person's circumstances.

### Ideal Learning Scenario

The life-context and timing in which a domain is best learned (*Shabbos* before *Shabbos*; *berachos* at the table). Held at the track grain, with item-level exceptions.

### Trajectory

The path a learner walks through the pool over time, composed from entry-level, track, and within-track rank.

---

## Sources

The first weighted corpus is built on the *Sefer HaMitzvot HaKatzar* of the Chofetz Chaim. The framework is **source-extensible** — additional halachic sources are layered in, each entry carrying its provenance and obligation level, with the same scoring, proportion, and personalization machinery applied across them.

## A Note on Use

> This is a study-prioritization aid, not a source of practical ruling. Any real-world question belongs to a competent halachic authority.
