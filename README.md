🌟 Vision

Halachah is vast. Acquiring real command of it takes years — often decades. Yet most learners cross that landscape without a map, without a guide, and without a system built around them.

The Halachic Mastery System changes that. We are building an intelligent, adaptive platform that:





Meets you where you are — assessing your present knowledge across the full halachic landscape



Builds your roadmap — from your background, your goals, and the realities of your life



Tests and adapts in real time — using modern learning science that responds to your progress



Weights what matters — driven by a carefully built map of halachah that reflects each area's frequency, severity, foundational importance, and interconnection

This is not another learning app. It is a mastery system — designed from the ground up to produce genuine, lasting command of halachah.

The technology is new. The aspiration is not — it is as old as Moshe's wish that all of God's people be capable in the law. See Foundations.



Status: active work in progress. The first weighted corpus is built; the platform, the adaptive engine, and the personalization layer are in development. Contributors welcome.



⭐ What makes it different

Most introductions to halacha move sequentially — one area, then the next, in an order set by a text's arrangement or a fixed syllabus. That order is learner-independent: it has nothing to do with how urgently you need a given halacha. So a person can spend months in early chapters and still not have reached the laws of speech, of money, or of the most common berachos — the halachos that govern dozens of decisions every day.

We dissolve that with four commitments:

















🧭 No waiting on chronology



Halachah is sorted into thematic tracks, and triage happens within each track. You advance through all of them in parallel — reaching the single most crucial matter in every area first.





⚖️ A weighted map, not a flat list



Every topic carries its real weight — frequency, severity, centrality, error-rate — so prioritization reflects what actually matters, not where a topic happens to sit.





📏 Proportion, taught not assumed



The gradations of obligation — d'oraysa, d'rabbanan, custom, ethical — and of severity stay visible, training a calibrated sense of concern: the biblical never treated lightly, the stringency never mistaken for the essence.





🎯 Personal, not merely leveled



The system adapts to your stage, your life (married, your field of work), and where you struggle — not a one-size roadmap.

A fifth idea runs quietly beneath: many formal mitzvos are rare as acts but constant as living forms. A formal oath is uncommon; casual swearing is everywhere. Affixing a mezuzah happens once; engaging it mindfully happens at every doorway. The map unbinds these — so the prevalent everyday practice is never buried under the rare formal act.



⚙️ How it works

flowchart LR
    A[1 · Intake<br/>background & goals] --> B[2 · Mapping<br/>your position]
    B --> C[3 · Roadmap<br/>your custom path]
    C --> D[4 · Learning<br/>structured content]
    D --> E[5 · Testing<br/>adaptive engine]
    E --> F[6 · Adaptation<br/>real-time re-route]
    F -. continuously .-> C

1 · Intake & assessment — a calibrated diagnostic, not a quiz, that maps what you know and how solidly you know it across halachic domains.

2 · Mapping — your results are placed against the full weighted map of halachah, locating exactly where you stand.

3 · Personalized roadmap — a custom path is generated from your position, your goals (daily practice, semicha prep, comprehensive mastery), your life circumstances, and the structural weight of each topic.

4 · Structured learning — content is delivered in the right order, with sources linked for every topic.

5 · Adaptive testing — assessment difficulty and focus shift with your performance, and review is scheduled by spaced-repetition science for long-term retention.

6 · Dynamic adaptation — the system watches your progress, surfaces weaknesses, and re-routes your roadmap automatically. No two learners follow the same path.



🗺️ The Halachah Map

At the core of the system is a comprehensive, weighted map of halachah — and it is one of the project's central contributions. Each topic is scored, not merely listed.

How weighting works. Every item is evaluated on seven independent axes, combined into a tunable score:







Axis



What it measures





Frequency (act)



How often the act is performed or the occasion arises





Ongoing relevance



How continuously it bears on life, even when the act is rare





Error-likelihood



How often people get it wrong in practice





Awareness-gap



How many don't realize it is a mitzvah or issur at all





Centrality



Foundational weight in Jewish life, independent of how well-known it is





Severity



Gravity of the prohibition (kares, misah, malkos, rabbinic)





Real-time stakes



Whether it must be acted on now, with no time to ask a rav

The weights are editable — change one and the whole map re-ranks. Items roll into tiers (Foundational → High → Moderate → Situational → Rare), each carries its level of obligation and its severity detail, and the map models prerequisite relationships so the system knows that mastering one area is the gateway to another.

Already built: the first weighted corpus — the full set of mitzvos in the Chofetz Chaim's Sefer HaMitzvot HaKatzar, scored on all seven axes, sorted into tracks and tiers, with the everyday derivatives surfaced as their own entries. It is the seed of the map and a working reference today. See [docs/METHODOLOGY.md](docs/METHODOLOGY.md).

🎯 Personalization

Sorting learners into levels is the floor, not the goal. The aim is to adapt to who a person actually is:





Stage — newcomer, building observance, maturing, comprehensive



Life — married brings family-purity and household halachos forward; a person in business needs choshen mishpat, ribbis, and honesty in trade nearer the front



Struggle — someone working on anger or speech has those surfaced first

The universal triage is the floor; personalization is the overlay that makes the path theirs. This is not a modern convenience grafted on — it is how the Ramchal describes the service of God itself. See below.



📜 Foundations

The system is an answer to ideas far older than any technology.



The Torah was meant to be everyone's.
Rav S.R. Hirsch, on morashah kehillas Yaakov (Devarim 33:4): the knowledge of the law was meant to be the shared inheritance of the entire community — every Jew rendered capable of consulting the sources himself for the guidance of daily life. Moshe's wish, "מִי יִתֵּן כָּל עַם ה' נְבִיאִים" — "would that all of God's people…" — was no idle phrase but a description of the goal.



Learn what you must live by.
Mesillas Yesharim (Introduction): it cannot be right that we exhaust our minds on investigations we were never obligated in and on pilpul that bears no fruit, while "מַה שֶּׁחַיָּבִים אָנוּ לְבוֹרְאֵנוּ … נַעַזְבֵהוּ לַהֶרְגֵּל" — leaving our great duty to our Creator to mere rote and habit.



Each according to who they are.
Mesillas Yesharim (ch. 26): piety is one and the same for everyone, but its path must differ — "כָּל אָדָם לְפִי הָאֻמָּנוּת אֲשֶׁר בְּיָדוֹ … כָּךְ צָרִיךְ לוֹ הַיְשָׁרָה וְהַדְרָכָה". The one whose occupation is Torah, the laborer, and the businessman each have their own road to the very same goal. And Hirsch (Bereishis 48): Israel's mission is bound to no single talent or walk of life — farmer, merchant, soldier, and scholar alike find their calling in one shared moral and spiritual task.



Order the practical law, and make it reachable.
The Rambam's project in the Mishneh Torah — to set out the whole of the practical law in clear, accessible order, so that a person can simply know what to do.

(One further teaching — that "each did what was right in his own eyes," usually a critique, can in the right light be praise for authentic, individualized avodah — we are still sourcing, and welcome help pinning it down.)

Together these define the mission: the practical law, made reachable, weighted by what matters, and shaped to the individual life.

💬 Philosophy



"Talmud Torah k'neged kulam" — the study of Torah stands against them all.

Every Jew deserves a system that honors the depth of halachah while meeting the reality of their life. Mastery should not depend on having had the right rebbeim, the right yeshiva, or the right background. With the right map and the right method, every serious learner can reach genuine command of the law — exactly as it was always meant to be.

Built with reverence for the tradition, and excitement for what careful technology now makes possible.
